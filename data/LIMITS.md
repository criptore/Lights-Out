# Limits: Storm Blackout and Restoration, version 1.2

Intended use: Comparing reported outages and restoration times across counties and storms, and studying how county night light changes after a storm.

Not intended for: Real-time emergency response, any decision about a single address or property, or statements about a tribal area drawn from county rows.

- Night light is related to electricity service but is a different quantity. Generators can restore light before the grid does, and damaged or empty buildings can stay dark after power returns. `status`, `days_to_50` and `days_to_normal` come from night light alone. Scored against reported outages, the result is weak. County mean darkness (dark_county_mean) does not separate counties with large outages from counties with small ones: pooled AUC 0.515 over 418 storms and 5,841 counties, 95% interval 0.482 to 0.547 from resampling storms. The interval includes 0.5, which is chance. Lit-pixel radiance drop (dark_lit_ratio) separates counties with large outages from counties with small ones: pooled AUC 0.559 over 287 storms and 3,611 counties, 95% interval 0.511 to 0.605 from resampling storms. Change in the share of unlit lit-mask pixels (dark_lit_share) separates counties with large outages from counties with small ones: pooled AUC 0.566 over 287 storms and 3,611 counties, 95% interval 0.513 to 0.615 from resampling storms. An AUC of 0.5 is a coin flip. Use the utility columns for outage size and restoration time.
- Radiance from different products should not be compared between storms; this release gives no conversion between them. 500 storms use VNP46A2 and 158 use VJ146A2 (`product`). Compare radiance within a storm, or between storms on the same product.
- County rows for tribal-area storms are context: they describe the whole county, not the tribal area, and tribal nations are sovereign areas. The share fields say how much of each county lies inside the area. The 47 tribal declarations have no county list, so they have tribal-area rows and overlapping-county context rows only and are absent from `county_summary.csv` and `county_daily.csv`. Their outage and customer columns are empty: EAGLE-I reports counties (`no_county_outage_record`) and EIA-861 customer counts are by county (`no_county_customer_count`). Outage records for the overlapping counties of 41 of these storms are in `tribal_context_utility_by_county.csv`, as context.
- Henri (AL082021) lists MA in `states` because of FEMA EM-3566-MA, an emergency declaration for the Mashpee Wampanoag Tribe that names no county. Its 14 Massachusetts rows in `county_summary.csv` are measured but not declared (`reason` is `county_not_declared`), and the tribal area, which lies in Barnstable and Bristol counties, is not measured for this storm.
- `thin_region` is True for a tribal area with fewer than 20 pixels on its tiles. `thin_night` is True for a county or area night with 1 to 19 valid pixels. Both keep the value as measured, and such values are noisy.
- 14 storms have no night-light numbers: 3 have a storm window that starts before the VNP46A2 record begins on 2012-01-19 (`before_nasa_record`), and 11 are in Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands or American Samoa, which the county boundary file does not cover (`territory_not_in_county_boundaries`).
- 195 storms have no outage run: 117 predate the public EAGLE-I county series, which starts 2014-11-01 (`no_utility_record_before_2014`). 30 fall in a year with no published EAGLE-I file (`no_published_year`). 41 are tribal declarations with no county named (`no_county_outage_record`). 7 are in territories EAGLE-I does not cover (`territory_not_in_eaglei`).
- 13 storms have an outage run but no county with EAGLE-I records in the window, so none of their `utility_by_county.csv` rows has `reason` `ok`: AL152017, DR-4227, DR-4271, DR-4275, DR-4296, DR-4306, DR-4311, DR-4327, DR-4388, DR-4571, DR-4609, DR-4649, DR-4805. Their rows give `no_utility_record` or `no_rows_in_window`, and their outage columns are empty.
- The 6 late-2025 storms (DR-4901, DR-4902, DR-4903, DR-4905, DR-4906, DR-4907) have an outage window that ends 31 December 2025, the last day of the published EAGLE-I record (`window_note` is `window_cut_at_source_end`). `outage_window_end_day` gives the planned window for these storms, and their records stop earlier.
- Customer-hours for counties in both version 1.1 and this table: Matthew (AL142016) sums to 140,943,783.1 in version 1.1 and 140,435,077.7 here (70 of 181 counties differ); Irma (AL112017) sums to 595,686,772.4 in version 1.1 and 586,489,025.7 here (144 of 379 counties differ).
- County pixels are placed half a pixel off: the first longitude and latitude of each tile are read as a pixel centre, where the file gives the tile's west and north edge. This release does not measure how many pixels land in a different county or how much county mean radiance would change. The numbers are kept as they are.
- Connecticut uses the eight 2020 counties; the 2024 file has nine planning regions. The storm records, the declared lists and the customer table use the old counties.
- Hurricane Eta stays as two rows. AL292020 is the NHC storm, and DR-4588 is FEMA's North Carolina disaster declaration titled Tropical Storm Eta. Every county declared for DR-4588 is also declared for AL292020. In those counties AL292020 dates the impact from the track, one day after the FEMA incident begin date that DR-4588 uses, so their night-light and outage values for those counties differ. `storms.csv` therefore has one row more than it has distinct storms. Count Eta once when counting storms.
- Bedford city, Virginia (51515) is declared but is not in the boundary file; it is listed in `declared_unmatched`. That table holds 4 rows, all for 51515: AL062018, AL182012, DR-4072, DR-4644. Their outage rows in `utility_by_county.csv` have an empty `county`.
- `in_nhc_wind_radius` is filled for the 34 storms with a HURDAT2 34-knot wind radius on file. Empty means no radius is on file for the storm. It does not mean the county was outside the radius.
- EAGLE-I coverage is incomplete in early years and in some states, and `customers_total` is an estimate: each state's EIA-861 customer count split across counties by Census housing units. Where the peak is above the estimate the peak is published as reported, and the share is empty with a reason.
- Where the 15-minute series does not reproduce the published peak, the restore hours are empty with reason `peak_mismatch` and the peak is left as published.
- The word outage still names night-light states: the `status` value `no_measurable_outage`, the columns `outage_min_ratio` and `outage_min_day`, and the reason code `no_outage_measured`. They describe the night-light series and say nothing about reported outages.
- `valid_pixel_share` and `snow_excluded_share` have different bases (all county pixels, and pixels with a finite radiance), so the two can add to more than 1.
